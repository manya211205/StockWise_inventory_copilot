# Phase 2: User Research, Persona & Jobs to Be Done

## 1. Project Overview

**Product:** StockWise — Inventory Decision Copilot  
**Target User:** Inventory and operations managers at small D2C brands  
**Product Type:** Rule-based inventory decision-support prototype

StockWise helps inventory managers identify replenishment risks, estimate order quantities, and prioritize purchases when their purchasing budget is limited.

This phase applies product-management concepts from the IIM Ahmedabad Product Mindset Casebook, focusing on understanding the user and their underlying needs before deciding what the product should build.

**Research status:** The persona, pain points, and user needs below are hypotheses. They have not yet been validated through interviews with actual inventory managers.

---

## 2. Problem Definition

Small direct-to-consumer (D2C) brands must balance product availability with limited working capital. Inventory managers need to decide which products to replenish, when to place orders, and how much inventory to purchase.

When inventory levels, sales information, supplier lead times, and incoming stock are difficult to evaluate together, these decisions can become time-consuming and inconsistent.

### Initial Problem Statement

Small D2C brand inventory and operations managers need a reliable way to identify replenishment priorities, determine appropriate order quantities, and allocate limited purchasing budgets so that they can reduce stockout risk without unnecessarily tying up cash in inventory.

### Potential User Problems

1. **Unexpected stockouts:** Products may run out before replacement stock arrives.
2. **Excess inventory:** Purchasing too much can tie up cash in slow-moving products.
3. **Unclear priorities:** Multiple products may require replenishment at the same time.
4. **Budget constraints:** The available purchasing budget may not cover every potential order.
5. **Manual analysis:** Managers may need to combine information from spreadsheets, sales reports, and supplier records.

These are initial problem hypotheses, not confirmed findings.

### Initial Focus Hypothesis

The most promising starting point is helping managers prioritize replenishment decisions when multiple products need attention but the purchasing budget is constrained.

This is a hypothesis to validate through user research.

## 3. Target User Persona

**Persona name:** Aditi Sharma (hypothetical)  
**Role:** Inventory and Operations Manager  
**Company:** A growing skincare D2C brand  
**Business model:** Sells through its website and online marketplaces

### Goals

- Keep high-demand products available.
- Reduce the risk of stockouts.
- Avoid excessive inventory of slow-moving products.
- Make purchasing decisions within budget.
- Explain replenishment decisions to the founder or finance team.

### Potential Frustrations

- Inventory and sales data may be spread across different spreadsheets or systems.
- It may be difficult to identify which products need immediate attention.
- Several products may appear urgent when purchasing cash is limited.
- Stakeholders may ask for lower spending without clearly defining which risks to accept.

### Constraints

- Limited working capital.
- Supplier lead times and possible minimum order quantities.
- Demand fluctuations and imperfect sales data.
- Limited time for manual inventory analysis.

### Persona Insight

Aditi does not simply need another inventory dashboard. She needs to make a defensible replenishment decision quickly, balancing product availability, purchasing costs, and financial constraints.

**Important:** This persona represents a research hypothesis. It must be refined using evidence from actual users.

## 4. Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

### Core Job Statement

"When I need to plan replenishment for my D2C brand and purchasing cash is limited, I want to identify which products are most urgent and estimate appropriate order quantities, so I can maintain product availability while controlling inventory spending."

### Three Dimensions of the Job

**Functional job**

Identify what to reorder, when to reorder it, and how much to purchase.

**Emotional job**

Feel confident that purchasing decisions are based on relevant information rather than guesswork.

**Social job**

Demonstrate to founders and other stakeholders that inventory spending decisions are reasoned and financially responsible.

### Situation and Trigger

The job may arise during a regular inventory review, when a supplier lead time changes, when a product approaches a stockout, or when several products require replenishment but the budget is insufficient for all of them.

These triggers need validation through interviews.

## 5. Existing Alternatives

Before adopting a new product, an inventory manager may rely on:

- Excel or Google Sheets.
- Reports from an existing inventory management system.
- Manual checks of stock levels and sales reports.
- Conversations with warehouse teams or suppliers.
- Personal experience and intuition.
- Delaying some purchases until more cash becomes available.

The objective is not to assume these alternatives are inadequate. Research must establish what users already do, what works well, and where meaningful gaps remain.

---

## 6. Switching Forces

### Push: Reasons to Seek an Alternative

Potential problems with the current process include time-consuming analysis, difficulty comparing products, and inconsistent prioritization.

### Pull: Reasons to Consider StockWise

A clearer prioritized action list, estimated order costs, and budget-aware recommendations could make replenishment decisions easier.

### Anxiety: Reasons to Hesitate

Users may distrust the calculations, worry about inaccurate recommendations, or be reluctant to upload sensitive business data.

### Habit: Reasons to Stay with the Current Approach

Spreadsheets and established systems are familiar, flexible, and already part of daily workflows.

### Product Implication

StockWise should not assume that displaying more information will create value. It needs to demonstrate that its recommendations improve a real decision, are understandable, and can be trusted.

## 7. User Research Plan

The next step is to interview inventory or operations managers at small D2C brands.

### Interview Questions

1. Tell me about the last time you had to decide which products to replenish.
2. How did you decide the order quantities, and what information did you use?
3. Tell me about a recent stockout or excess inventory situation. What caused it?
4. What happens when several products need replenishment but you cannot afford all the purchases?
5. What tools or workarounds do you use today, and what is the most frustrating part of the process?

### Research Principles

- Ask about real past experiences rather than hypothetical preferences.
- Understand the current workflow before proposing a solution.
- Ask for examples, decisions, consequences, and workarounds.
- Record recurring problems separately from isolated incidents.
- Do not treat a user's interest in a proposed feature as proof of demand.

### Evidence to Collect

For each interview, document the user's role, business context, recent example, existing process, pain points, consequences, and any evidence that challenges the initial hypotheses.

Do not invent interview findings or present assumptions as research results.

## 8. Initial Product Implications

Based on the current hypotheses, StockWise could explore the following capabilities:

- A prioritized list of products requiring attention.
- Transparent explanations for each recommendation.
- Estimated replenishment quantities and order costs.
- Budget-aware purchasing scenarios.
- Clear warnings when input data is incomplete or assumptions may affect recommendations.

These are candidate capabilities, not a finalized feature roadmap. Their priority should depend on research evidence and user value.

---

## 9. Key Questions to Resolve

Before expanding the product, research should establish:

1. Is constrained-budget replenishment a frequent and significant problem?
2. How do managers currently calculate stockout risk and order quantities?
3. Which inputs are essential for trustworthy recommendations?
4. How do supplier lead times, minimum order quantities, and demand variability affect decisions?
5. What would make managers trust or reject a recommendation?
6. How should success be measured in a real business setting?

---

## 10. Current Conclusion

StockWise is a working rule-based prototype, while the product problem and proposed user needs remain to be validated.

The current direction is to investigate whether helping small D2C inventory managers prioritize replenishment under budget constraints solves a meaningful problem.

The next phase will translate validated user needs into product requirements, define the minimum viable product (MVP), and prioritize features using an explicit framework.
